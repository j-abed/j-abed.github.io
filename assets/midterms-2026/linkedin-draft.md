## Post

The forecasts anticipated Democratic gains. The harder question was how far those gains would reach.

In the supplied November 4 snapshot, the typical House district shifted 14.3 points toward Democrats relative to its 2024 presidential margin. That movement was unusually widespread across districts with very different political histories.

One simple model makes the distinction clear: pre-election inputs imply 243 Democratic seats; the swing observed after the election implies 262. A 19-seat difference. Same model, different information. These are model estimates, not official results.

Using the observed swing, the rule correctly classifies 95.7% of the 392 called House races. That is a retrospective, in-sample fit, not a pre-election forecasting record.

The challenge wasn't just predicting who would win. It was estimating the size of the shift. The infographic summarizes this preliminary analysis; election totals and external source attributions remain unverified.
